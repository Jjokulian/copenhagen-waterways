# L3 — The trend depends on the start year

*Test draft. Status: procedure specified and run once on bed oxygen; numbers below are that run, not a literature quote.*

## The observable consequence, stated so it can fail

L3 has a weak reading and a strong reading, and only the strong one is a claim about the sea.

**Weak:** *a slope fitted to a Danish series changes when you move the cut.* This is true of every noisy series ever fitted and is not worth testing — see the null below, which produces it 100% of the time from nothing.

**Strong:** *the window-dependence exceeds what a single constant rate plus the series' own autocorrelated noise would produce* — i.e. the record contains genuinely different eras, so "the trend" is not a well-defined quantity and reporting one number is a category error.

**Falsified if:** over the full space of admissible windows, the spread of fitted slopes, and the fraction of them that are positive, both fall inside the interval generated by simulating one constant slope with matched AR(1) noise. Then the window-dependence is noise around a single rate, the number is estimable, and L3's strong reading is not established for this variable.

**Supported if:** the observed surface is more dispersed, or its sign-share further from a single-trend expectation, than that simulation allows.

## Data, counted

`docs/data/areas/stations_series.{json,bin}` — 679,422 station-months (verified: `sum(n)` over the 9 variables; `stations_series.bin` is 5,435,376 bytes, exactly the declared layout), 1,415 stations, month index 0–560 = 1980-01 to 2026-09.

Variable used: **`oxy_bed`**, near-bed oxygen in mg/l, **76,980** rows. Dropped **472** as impossible (432 at ≤ 0 mg/l, 40 above 20 mg/l; the raw range is −9.93 to 281.6) — **error class 1, value error**. Kept **76,508** rows on **1,325** stations.

Two further error classes are structural and are not fixed, only declared. The archive is already a **monthly median per station** — **class 3, aggregation**: within-month spread and the diel oxygen cycle are gone before I see the file, and there is **no time-of-day column anywhere** (**class 6, absent dimension**). Both inflate the year-to-year residual; the null below is matched to the residual actually observed, so they widen the null rather than being smuggled into the signal. The saturation cross-check uses `oxysat_bed`, which is computed from oxygen, temperature and salinity — **class 7, model-as-datum** — and is reported as a robustness check only.

## The constraint, and therefore the null

Constraint imposed: annual index years **1989–2025** (37 years; before 1989 fewer than 200 stations report per year, 2026 is a part-year of 819 rows), windows of **≥ 10 years**, giving **406 admissible windows**. Index = two-way fixed effects (station + calendar month, alternating demeaning to convergence), then the annual mean of residuals. Station effects absorb level differences, so composition change in *levels* cannot drive the index.

**The null is not "zero sensitivity".** For each station set I estimate ρ and σ from the detrended index, then simulate 4,000 AR(1) series of the same length and compute the same 406-window surface. Two nulls:

- **H0, no trend at all.** Sign-flipping windows occur in **100.0%** of simulations, in every station set. Median slope range 0.104 mg/l/yr (pooled). *A sign flip carries no information whatsoever.*
- **H1, one constant slope equal to the fitted full-window slope, plus matched noise.** This is the null that L3's strong reading must beat.

Slopes in mg/l/yr. Fitted slopes run from **−0.054 to +0.084** (core set) — a 15-fold range crossing zero. With the end fixed at 2025 every start year gives a negative slope (−0.0085 at 1989, −0.054 at 2016); with the start fixed at 1989 the slope is **+0.084** ending 1998 and **−0.0085** ending 2025. **The sign is set by the end year, not the start year.**

**Every dispersion statistic is consistent with H1.** P(H1 range ≥ observed) is 0.36–0.44 everywhere: one constant rate plus this series' own autocorrelation reproduces the whole surface.

## The two confounds

**Composition.** The summer-peaked share of `oxy_bed` station-months falls from 0.126 (1989–94) to 0.035 (2015–19) — the compositional shift is real and large in this variable. It is handled by re-running on a fixed 186-station core panel and on the 427 year-round stations separately; both give the same answer, and the core panel gives a *stronger* directional signal, not a weaker one. My Rayleigh classification (426 summer / 427 year-round) is computed on `oxy_bed` rows only and does not reproduce the project's 293/481 — treat the split as re-derived, not inherited.

**Thermometer.** Repeating on `oxysat_bed` (64,493 rows, 449 dropped): the fraction of positive windows rises from 0.11–0.15 to **0.28–0.30**, and the full-window slope is −0.015 %sat/yr. Moving from a concentration to a saturation ruler roughly doubles the sign ambiguity. Consistent with part of the mg/l decline being solubility, not oxygen supply. Not separated here.

## What this licenses

Licensed: for near-bed oxygen in this archive, **L3's strong reading is not established**. The window-dependence is fully reproduced by one constant slope with matched autocorrelation, and the interesting asymmetry is the opposite of L3's framing — start year barely matters, end year decides the sign.

Not licensed: nothing about chlorophyll, nutrient loading, or the modelled reference condition, which is where L3's stake actually lies (L1, L2). Nothing about pre-1980, which is the era L3 is really about — this archive starts at 1980, after the loading peak, so it cannot test "a trend measured from a peak". Nothing causal. And a negative here does not license reporting a single slope: the fitted rate still ranges −0.054 to +0.084, so any published number needs its window and its H1 interval printed beside it.
