# G1 — Warming

Draft only. Counts verified by reading the files; the pilot slopes below **carry no null**,
so none is a result.

## The claim, and why it needs splitting before it can be tested

G1 predicts "monotone worsening independent of load", and offers that as why load reduction
has not produced recovery. But warming reaches near-bed oxygen by three routes —
**solubility** (deterministic, Weiss 1970), **stratification** (C1's object) and **metabolic
rate** (Q10, unmeasured here) — only the first of which is policy-inert. So G1's testable
content is not *does oxygen fall* but **what fraction of the long-term fall is
thermodynamics**: the seasonal decomposition done here (23-fold → 14-fold on a saturation
threshold, ~40 % thermodynamic), moved onto the long-term axis.

## Observable consequence, stated to be falsified

Per station-month, `C_sat` = Weiss (1970) from near-bed T and S, mL/L × 1.42905:

> `O_bed = C_sat(T_bed, S_bed) − D`,  so  `dO/dt ≡ dC_sat/dt − dD/dt`.

`C_sat` is exact and external, so the first term is **computed, not estimated**. The deficit
`D` is solubility-neutral by construction and carries the rest: consumption, failed
ventilation, advection.

**G1-a:** *the decline is not mostly solubility* — `dC_sat/dt` is a minority of `dO/dt`.
Falsified if the solubility term equals or exceeds the observed decline.

**G1-b:** *`dD/dt > 0`* — the deficit widens, as it must if warming drives respiration or
stratification faster than ventilation recovers. Falsified by `dD/dt ≤ 0`, or by a `dD/dt`
indistinguishable from the year-block null. G1-b carries the load-independence claim; G1-a
decides whom the finding is about.

## Data, with verified counts

`docs/data/areas/stations_series.{json,bin}` — 679,422 station-months, 1,415 stations, months
0–560 (Jan 1980 – Sep 2026). Five-way join `oxy_bed ∩ temp_bed ∩ sal_bed ∩ temp_surf ∩
sal_surf`: **76,309**; after plausibility (0 ≤ O ≤ 20, −2 ≤ T ≤ 30, 0 ≤ S ≤ 40 at both
depths) **76,264 rows, 1,314 stations, 1980–2026**.

Trend panel = cells (station × calendar month) with ≥ 8 distinct years **and** ≥ 15-year
span: **2,429 cells · 54,211 rows · 310 stations**. Balanced variant (cell in both 1990–99
and 2015–26): 1,282 cells · 35,215 rows. Rows per half-decade fall 14,491 (1990–94) → 6,842
(2020–24): the network thins, which is the composition problem.

**Error classes.** (1) value — `oxy_bed` −9.93 to 281.6 mg/l, `temp_surf` to 80.3 °C; 45 rows
removed. (2) quantisation — one-decimal T; ±0.05 °C is ±0.015 mg/l in `C_sat`, which
matters for a single value, not a slope. (3) aggregation — monthly medians, so
`D` may pair T, S and O from different casts. (5) unfilled — 12,736 `oxy_bed` cells carry no
archived saturation. (6) **no time-of-day column anywhere** (`ctd.csv.gz` has `Dato` as
`19701013`); if sampling hour drifted over 47 years, the diel oxygen cycle drifted with it,
uncorrectably — the one class that caps this test rather than bounds it. (7) avoided: the
unit is a station, and no water body is needed, every estimate being within-cell.

## Circularity, avoided

Archived `oxysat_bed` is computed from O, T and S, so it already contains temperature.
**It is never an outcome here.** `D` contains T through `C_sat` deliberately — that is the
*subtraction* of the solubility channel, Weiss being external to the archive; and errors in
T enter `D` but not the year regressor, so they add noise, not trend. Worked through in
[`C6.md`](C6.md); not restated.

## The null, computed under the constraint actually imposed

Not "zero slope". The estimator is a within-cell FE slope on year; the constraint imposed is
*within station and within calendar month*. Under it I measured the dependence the textbook
SE ignores:

- Autocorrelation of within-cell detrended `D` residuals at annual spacing: lag-1 mean
  **−0.048**, lag-2 **−0.057** over 2,312 cells — indistinguishable from detrending's own
  −1/(n−1) ≈ −0.05 bias. **Temporal AR is not the problem here.**
- The dependence is *cross-sectional*: SD of the year-mean residual **0.2513** where
  independence predicts **0.0471** — variance inflation **28.4**. All cells in a year sample the same weather.

So the null is a **year-block procedure**: resample the 47 calendar years with replacement,
and separately permute year labels attached to whole years; refit, 2,000 draws. That keeps
every station effect, the seasonal cycle and the within-year common shock, destroying only
the year→outcome ordering. Consequence: the naive SE on `dD/dt` (0.00061 mg/l/yr)
understates by ≈ √28.4 ≈ **5.3×**, to ≈ 0.0032. A quoted null would have been wrong by that
factor.

## Composition, handled

The phenology mix moves. On my own definition (≥ 8 `oxy_bed` months; ≥ 65 % in May–Oct →
"summer-peaked"): **282 summer-peaked vs 480 year-round**, the summer-peaked running
**1.25 mg/l lower** in May–Oct (6.31 vs 7.56, d **0.50**, n = 12,390 / 32,924), their share
of station-months moving **29.5 % (2010–14) → 16.4 % (2015–19)**. Direction and size reproduce the figure
on file (293/481, 1.57 mg/l, d 0.66, 32→12 %); the split is threshold-dependent, which is
itself the warning.

Three defences, all required: (i) within-cell FE, so a station only competes with itself;
(ii) the balanced panel, so entry and exit cannot tilt the slope; (iii) phenology × year.

## Procedure

1. Read the five arrays at the `.json` offsets, key `station×1000 + month`, filter → 76,264;
   compute `C_sat` (Weiss) and `D`; build the ≥ 8-year / ≥ 15-span panel.
2. FE slopes on year for `O`, `C_sat`, `D`, `T_bed`, `T_surf − T_bed`. The first three satisfy
   the identity exactly; report the split as a **fraction of `dO/dt`**, not a p-value.
3. Year-block null (above) for each; report naive SE, block SE and their ratio.
4. Repeat balanced, Jun–Sep, Dec–Mar, and with the phenology interaction. **Agreement across
   all five is the finding; disagreement is equally the finding.**
5. Refit `dD/dt` with surface-minus-bed density as a covariate — this *uses* C1's variable and
   does not test it. What survives is the metabolic-plus-unknown residue, a residue not a Q10.

### What the pilot already shows (no null attached — not yet a result)

On the 2,429-cell panel, per decade: `T_bed` **+0.42 °C**, `C_sat` **−0.099 mg/l**, `O_bed`
**−0.046 mg/l**, `D` **−0.053 mg/l**. The solubility term exceeds the whole observed
decline; the deficit appears to *narrow*. But `dD/dt` sits ~1.6 block-SE from zero and moves
to −0.031 (balanced), −0.088 (Jun–Sep), −0.040 (Dec–Mar) — spread wider than its own
uncertainty. Surface-minus-bed temperature difference **−0.027 °C/decade**: no detectable
strengthening of thermal stratification here.

## What a result would and would not license

**Would.** If `dC_sat/dt` matches or exceeds `dO/dt` under the year-block null and across all
five sensitivities, that licenses: *at these 310 stations the long-term near-bed oxygen
decline is accounted for by the solubility ceiling alone, and the non-thermodynamic deficit
did not widen.* That constrains G1's headline directly: "monotone worsening independent of
load" would be true of the water and still not evidence that load reduction failed, the
falling term being the one no load policy touches.

**Would not.** It does not measure respiration and cannot: `D` is a standing stock, Q10 a
rate, and converting between them needs a ventilation timescale the archive lacks (class 6).
A narrowing `D` is equally consistent with load reduction working *and* with the network
drifting toward better-ventilated positions — (i)–(iii) bound that, they do not eliminate
it. It says nothing about stratification beyond a two-point difference at sampling depth, which
is no pycnocline (C1's object; believe C1 here), nothing about water bodies, nothing about
the 1,004 stations the span filter drops. And `dD/dt ≈ 0` refutes G1-b only at the
resolution of monthly medians taken at an unrecorded hour.
