## The specific case: the chain behind the nitrogen figure

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LR-L-CHAIN">A field surplus becomes a modelled load, becomes a percentage, gets allocated to a water body, is judged against a threshold, and shows a trend. Every link is public and examined here. **Two do not hold, and a third rests on less than it appears to.**</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LR-L-CHAIN "What this claim rests on")</sup>

- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LR-L-FIELD">**No field is measured.** The *markoverskud* that drives the model is a balance — nitrogen supplied to the fields minus nitrogen harvested — computed from registers and statistics, not measured in any field. Change a rule and the number moves with no change in any field: in 1999 the nitrogen norm was cut by [10](SOURCES.md#F-c889b9ac7d)%, and the national report publishes 2013 twice, at [84](SOURCES.md#F-8310e3e2ec) and [81](SOURCES.md#F-b4ecc40fb3) kg N/ha, depending on which register supplied the mineral-fertiliser term.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LR-L-FIELD "What this claim rests on")</sup> [the construction, term by term →](NITROGEN.md)
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LR-L-CIRCULAR">**One published figure rests on less than it appears to.** The national stream report plots the field surplus against the normalised diffuse nitrogen load and calls the relation very strong and significant; the same report then notes that not all fjord catchments respond equally quickly, Mariager Fjord among them. Whether the modelled part of that load is itself computed from the surplus is the question [what the sea actually receives](NITROGEN.md) takes up.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LR-L-CIRCULAR "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LR-L-BOUNDARIES">**The boundaries carry no measured signal.** Across all [255](SOURCES.md#F-16416eede3) feature subsets of the station record, the official water bodies agree with the partitions the data produce no better than random connected regions of the same sizes agree with each other — lift [-0.026](SOURCES.md#F-cc1d7f1b8e) to [-0.014](SOURCES.md#F-fe6a50860b) against that contiguity-matched null.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LR-L-BOUNDARIES "What this claim rests on")</sup> [how that was tested →](AREAS.md)

Each link above has its working, its null and its limits in [what the sea actually receives](NITROGEN.md). And for the question that follows — *so where, and what would actually help?* — [Places, not categories](PLACES.md) takes the coasts one at a time. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LR-L-PLACES">In near-bed oxygen, one region is below [2](SOURCES.md#F-dbc579d4ab) mg/l [3.9](SOURCES.md#F-2843de66c9) times as often as the next.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LR-L-PLACES "What this claim rests on")</sup>
