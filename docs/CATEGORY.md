# The category that was never measured

That page is about a *number* nobody measured being treated as one. This is about a *category* nobody measured being treated as one.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-CAT-COMPANION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## The tell

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-TELL">Open the Danish marine monitoring archive this project holds. Its measured columns come with what a measurement comes with: a sonde serial number, a sampling instrument, an analysis method, a laboratory, a quality level, and a mark for a value below what the method could detect.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-TELL "What this claim rests on")</sup>

Now look at the categorical columns. Water body `DKCOAST2`. Citizenship `DK`. Sector `C10.51`. Diagnosis `E11.9`. Species *Fucus vesiculosus*. Land use `arable`.

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-NO-ERRORBAR">**Not one of them has an error bar, and not one of them was measured.** Each is the output of a model — a polygon someone drew, a legal test someone applied, a classification someone wrote down — presented in the same table, in the same typeface, as the quantities that came off an instrument. The instrument reading is an observation with a known error. The category is a claim with an unstated one.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-NO-ERRORBAR "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## What the category is actually doing

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-TWO-THINGS">Every basket carries two things that are easy to conflate, and the conflation is the whole problem:</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-TWO-THINGS "What this claim rests on")</sup>

1. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-MEMBERSHIP">**A membership rule.** Who is in. This is usually sound,
   often precise, and frequently a genuine fact about the world. Roskilde Fjord really is
   an enclosure. Danish citizenship really is a determinate legal status. A postcode
   really is a postcode.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-MEMBERSHIP "What this claim rests on")</sup>
2. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-ATTRIBUTION">**An attribution.** What members are taken to share. This is a
   *statistical* claim, and nothing in the membership rule establishes it.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-ATTRIBUTION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-QUESTION">The name serves both functions, so the switch between them goes unnoticed. Asking "is this a real category?" answers the first question and is almost always yes. The question that has content is different:</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-QUESTION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

> **Does membership predict the thing you are using it to predict — and does it
> predict better than a same-shaped arbitrary grouping would?**

## The measurement

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-ICC-FAMILY">There is a standard family of statistics for this: the **intraclass correlation** and its relatives. Pick two members of the population at random. If they are in the same basket, how much more alike are they than two picked without regard to basket? For the intraclass correlation, one means membership tells you everything and zero means it tells you nothing. The scores this project computes are a relative — a ratio of mean squares, on which no information reads near one half rather than zero — which is one more reason to read only their difference from a null.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-ICC-FAMILY "What this claim rests on")</sup>

Two things make it usable rather than decorative.

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-CONDITION">**Condition on what everything shares.** Every marine station in Denmark experiences the same season. Pool across months and the seasonal signal lands in the between-basket term and *every* partition scores well — including a deliberately absurd one. The same applies wherever a common driver exists: compare within year, within cohort, within whatever the members hold in common regardless of basket.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-CONDITION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SHAPE-NULL">**Compare against a null that matches the basket's shape.** This is the part that gets skipped, and skipping it is how a defensible number comes out backwards.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SHAPE-NULL "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## The statistic is a model too, and on its own it is circular

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-DEFECT">The measurement above has a defect that has to be stated before the worked example, because it applies to every number on this page.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-DEFECT "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-FST-CIRCLE">**The intraclass correlation takes the partition as an input.** So does $F_{\mathrm{ST}}$, a statistic of the same family in population-genetics clothing: $F_{\mathrm{ST}} = (H_T - H_S)/H_T$, and $H_S$ — the variation *within subpopulations* — cannot be computed until the subpopulations have been declared. Citing an $F_{\mathrm{ST}}$ value as evidence that populations are distinct therefore assumes what it sets out to show, unless it is set against what partitions not chosen to be real would give.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-FST-CIRCLE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SAMPLING">The partition can come from the **sampling design** itself. Collect fifty Danes and fifty Dutch and "Danish" and "Dutch" are your groups by construction; the statistic then reports on a boundary the fieldwork drew.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SAMPLING "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-ESTIMATORS">Nor is it one statistic. After Wright's, papers gave differing definitions and estimators, and published estimates for the same pairs of populations have moved more than twofold with the estimation method, the way estimates are combined across loci and the choice of loci. It is an aggregation function over a chosen metric, presented as an observable.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-ESTIMATORS "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-RESCUE">**This is why the null is not optional: without it the measurement cannot even show that the grouping beats noise - and beating noise is still not a shared cause.**</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-RESCUE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-DIFFERENCE">Compared against a *shape-matched null*, a circular statistic at least stops rewarding a partition for its shape: the **difference** says how far the chosen partition sits above noise of the same shape. That is a floor, not a test that its members share a cause - a random basket shares none, so beating it shows only that the grouping beats noise (statistical-methods, section 6b). It is also why a single reported value — a bare $F_{\mathrm{ST}}$, a bare intraclass correlation, a bare $R^2$ between groups — cannot by itself say whether the groups are anything.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-DIFFERENCE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## Three nulls, three answers — a worked example

Denmark's marine water bodies, scored for [9](SOURCES.md#F-8f9b6536e3) measured variables, within month:

| null | what it holds fixed | what it destroys | which variable gains most |
|---|---|---|---|
| shuffled labels | basket sizes | geography entirely | salinity, by a distance |
| latitude stripes | compactness, equal sizes | hydrography | surface oxygen saturation |
| size- and shape-matched blobs | **both** sizes and compactness | hydrography only | surface oxygen saturation |

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-WINNERS">The winners are read from the scores, and the first two disagree, not marginally: surface oxygen saturation gains **[+0.065](SOURCES.md#F-828940ba27)** over the shuffle and **[+0.408](SOURCES.md#F-288622cc55)** over the stripes, while against the shuffle salinity gains most.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-WINNERS "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-REVERSAL">Only the third varies one thing at a time, and against it the ordering from the first **reverses** — oxygen saturation gains most, salinity little — and fluorescence's lift is below zero but inside the spread of the three blob runs, so for fluorescence the official partition does no better than random compact blobs.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-REVERSAL "What this claim rests on")</sup>

The reading that survives is not about which variable matters:

> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-LIFT-RULE">**Beating its own shape is the least a partition must do: a grouping that predicts no better than random groups of the same shape adds nothing to its outline. Beating them is a floor, not proof that its members share a cause.**</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-LIFT-RULE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-READING">A reading was offered here: if salinity is spatially smooth, *any* compact grouping predicts it and real boundaries add little, while boundaries that follow enclosure carry information about oxygen. The co-movement test on [AREAS.md](AREAS.md) does not bear it out: at matched distance the water-body lines add about as much for salinity as for bed oxygen, so they follow water masses rather than separating an oxygen process.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-READING "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## Two methods, and the condition that chooses between them

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-NULL-LIMIT">The null comparison above is not the strongest test available. It is the strongest test available *when the feature space is small*.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-NULL-LIMIT "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-NULL-CONDITIONED">A null answers: **given these measurements, does this grouping beat chance?** The answer is still conditioned on which measurements were taken, so it cannot separate a property of the entities from an artifact of what someone chose to record.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-NULL-CONDITIONED "What this claim rests on")</sup>

There is a test that can, and it assumes no partition at all:

> Cluster the entities using a **random subset** of the measured features.
> Cluster them again using a **disjoint** random subset.
> Ask how much the two agree — and whether agreement **rises** as the subsets grow.

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SPLIT-TEST">A grouping that appears only when one particular measurement is included is an artifact of that measurement. A grouping that appears whichever features are drawn is a property of the entities. Convergence as the subsets widen is the signal; flat agreement near chance means there is nothing to converge on. The official partition is then scored on the same axis — its agreement with each derivation — so it is one candidate among them rather than the standard they are marked against.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SPLIT-TEST "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SMALL-SPACE">A feature space of three or four measurements cannot be split into disjoint halves: there is nothing to hold out, and a null is the only test available.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SMALL-SPACE "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-WHERE-MANY">Where many measurements accumulate on the same entities, the stronger test is available and the weaker one is a habit.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-WHERE-MANY "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-THRESHOLD">**So the rule is a threshold, not a preference.** Below [12](SOURCES.md#F-d758cf776f) independent features — a judgment, stated as one — use a shape-matched null and accept that it cannot distinguish the entities from the instrument. Above that, split the feature space, because you can.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-THRESHOLD "What this claim rests on")</sup>

*<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-OWN-EXAMPLE">This page's own worked example sits below that threshold — [9](SOURCES.md#F-8f9b6536e3) variables, so [4](SOURCES.md#F-b7a76fa5b4) per half, and far from independent: oxygen and its saturation, the same quantity at two depths — which is why the marine result is suggestive only. The method wants a case with dozens of characters per entity, which is what `../organism` exists to try.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-OWN-EXAMPLE "What this claim rests on")</sup>*

## You have read this category in other clothes

One line each, with no claim that any is wrong — only that each has a membership rule doing one job and an attribution doing another:

- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-CITIZENSHIP">**Citizenship.** A legal status, acquired by the routes a
  country's law sets out. It can be used as a proxy for population, culture, ancestry,
  or behaviour — none of which the membership rule tracks. The rule is *legal*; the
  attribution is *biological or cultural*.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-CITIZENSHIP "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-RACE">**Race.** A dispute in the literature turns on exactly this
  statistic. By Edwards's account it is often stated, after Lewontin in 1972, that about
  [85](SOURCES.md#F-1697485954)% of human genetic variation lies within populations and only [15](SOURCES.md#F-02635cc76a)%
  between them. Edwards (2003) replied that the information distinguishing populations
  lies in the correlations between loci, so classification can be accurate anyway — the
  shape of the compact-null finding above, where compact groupings of a smooth field
  score well wherever they are cut. Rosenberg et al. (2002) found clusters without using
  the individuals' origins; Serre & Pääbo (2004), sampling individuals homogeneously
  across the globe, found gradients rather than discrete clusters. Sampling design,
  again.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-RACE "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SPECIES">**Species.** Species concepts agree that a species is a
  separately evolving lineage but disagree about which further property makes one —
  reproductive isolation, diagnosability, monophyly — so the same organisms can be
  sorted differently. Which concept to use is argued over; which one predicts a given
  trait is a question with an answer.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SPECIES "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-SECTOR">**Industrial sector** (SIC, NACE). A code attached to each
  firm from a classification; productivity and emissions can then be compared "within
  sector".</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-SECTOR "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-ICD">**Diagnosis codes.** For more than a century the basis for
  comparable statistics on causes of death and disease, and now also used for
  resource allocation and reimbursement — a membership rule built for counting, used
  to stand for a condition.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-ICD "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-POSTCODE">**Deprivation by postcode.** An area statistic attributed to
  the individuals in it — which is the ecological fallacy, and the same error
  [AREAS.md](AREAS.md) reports for Denmark.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-POSTCODE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## What to demand instead

Three questions, and they are cheap:

1. **What is the membership rule?** Say it out loud. Legal, geometric,
   administrative, self-reported?
2. **What is being attributed to members?** And is that the same kind of thing as
   the rule?
3. **What is the lift over a same-shaped null?** Not the raw statistic — the raw
   statistic took the category as an input and cannot testify about it. The lift.

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-AR-DEMAND">If the answer to the third is unknown, the category is being used as data when it is a model, and any figure computed inside it is a statement about the basket at least as much as about the world.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-AR-DEMAND "What this claim rests on")</sup>
