# What the sea actually receives

Generated by `scripts/nitrogen.py`. Reproducible from `data/raw` and `data/manual`.

The figure in circulation is that agriculture accounts for **69.6% of nitrogen**. This document does not dispute the measurements behind it. It asks three questions the percentage does not survive.

## 1. There is no denominator

A percentage requires a closed set. Here is every pathway of reactive nitrogen to Danish marine waters we could enumerate.

| Pathway | kt N/yr | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Danish land via monitored streams | 32 – 38 | partly measured — 49% of area; grab samples + linear interpolation, documented low bias |
| Danish land via unmonitored catchments | 27 – 33 | modelled — 51% of area; diffuse model minus modelled lake/wetland retention |
| **Foreign rivers to shared Danish waters** | **—** | unquantified — e.g. German inflow to Flensborg Fjord; not separated in Danish accounts |
| Treatment plants (in the stream total or direct) | 2.5 – 3.5 | measured — flow-metered, routinely sampled - best-constrained line |
| Rain-dependent overflow, combined sewer | 0.2 – 4 | modelled — 0.4 kt at published typetal; upper bound allows first flush + scour + volume error |
| Separate stormwater outfalls | 0.2 – 2 | modelled — concentration from a 1990 programme, applied nationally |
| Scattered dwellings | 0.05 – 0.3 | modelled — dwelling counts x assumed per-capita load |
| Industry | 0.2 – 0.5 | measured — reported |
| Marine aquaculture (net pens) | 0.3 – 1 | modelled — feed balance; direct to sea |
| Atmospheric deposition to Danish sea, wet+dry | 45 – 65 | modelled — DEHM output; 53 kt in 2022, 89 kt in 2014 - no direct over-water measurement |
| **Organic N in deposition (DON)** | **—** | unquantified — routinely excluded from deposition inventories |
| **Shipping NOx deposited locally** | **—** | unquantified — inside the deposition total but not separated |
| **Submarine groundwater discharge** | **—** | unquantified — NOT A TERM in the national account; monitored only at 1.5-5 m depth in 6 catchments |
| **Legacy N still in transit** | **—** | unquantified — 8-24 yr lag observed in LOOP; a timing term on the rows above, not an extra source |
| **Drained organic soils / lowmoor peat** | **—** | unquantified — inside the diffuse residual, not separated |
| Net advective import, Kattegat + Belt | 60 – 180 | modelled — small net of two very large opposing fluxes; older studies; highly variable |
| **N2 fixation in situ** | **—** | unquantified — not quantified for Danish waters |
| **Coastal and seabed erosion** | **—** | unquantified — organic N release |
| **Dredging / port resuspension** | **—** | unquantified —  |
| **Sediment efflux / internal regeneration** | **—** | unquantified — one study puts net advection at ~10% of what annual primary production requires,      which implies regeneration supplies most of it; state-dependent, rises under anoxia |

**10 of 20 pathways carry no number at all**, including what is probably the largest supply to the productive layer — internal regeneration from sediment, which is not a "source" and so has no row in any apportionment.

The quantified subset sums to **167 – 327 kt N/yr**. That is not a range for the total. With ten pathways empty the honest statement is:

> Total reactive nitrogen supply **≥ 167 kt N/yr**, with no established upper bound.

A lower bound, not a range. And therefore no denominator, and no percentages — until the empty rows are filled.

Note the scale of what the apportionment omits. Atmospheric deposition to Danish marine waters is 45–90 kt N/yr, comparable to the entire land-based waterborne term, and appears in none of the published shares.

## 2. The numbers are not estimators of what they are named

Each line of the published apportionment, relabelled by what actually produced it:

| Called | Actually is |
|---|---|
| **Agriculture, 69.6%** | **Residual.** (grab-sampled load over 49% of the area + model output over 51%) minus modelled point sources minus modelled natural background, with retention modelled at ±6-27 percentage points. |
| **Natural background, 22.1%** | Model output — and the subtrahend that determines the residual above. |
| **Treatment plants, 4.8%** | **Reported effluent monitoring.** Flow-metered, routinely sampled. The best-measured line in the table. |
| **Aquaculture and marine farms, 1.5%** | Reported, feed-balance based. |
| **Separate stormwater, 0.7%** | Modelled volume × a concentration from a **1990** measurement programme. |
| **Rain-dependent overflow, 0.6%** | Modelled volume (30-135% stated uncertainty) × an assumed concentration, quality-controlled **against that same assumed concentration**. |
| **Industry, 0.5%** | Reported. |
| **Scattered dwellings, 0.2%** | Modelled from dwelling counts × assumed per-capita loads. |

Two of these are measurements. The rest are models, and the largest is a residual of models.

### The validation is circular

Reported nitrogen divided by reported volume must fall within **10–12 mg/l** for combined sewers and **1.8–2.2 mg/l** for separate ones. Outside that, *"kontaktes den dataansvarlige med henblik på at få rettet eventuelle fejl"* — the data owner is contacted to correct the error.

The check tests conformity to the assumed concentration, not accuracy. A genuinely high measured first-flush concentration would be flagged as a mistake. **The assumed concentration cannot be falsified by data collected under it.**

### And the estimator can return impossible values

In dry years such as 1996 and 2005 the calculated cultivation contribution comes out NEGATIVE - described in the literature as a meaningless result caused by the uncertainty in the assessment. A residual whose error can exceed its own signal is published to one decimal place with no error bar.

Underneath it: **240 stream stations** covering **49%** of the country, the other **51%** modelled. Load is grab samples plus linear interpolation, and in all three streams of the 2018 validation study that method **always underestimated** — annual deviations -0.9% to -7.7%, monthly deviations reaching +98%. Retention, the largest single term, carries **±6–27 percentage points**.

### Overflow is modelled, and the deciding variable is not collected

| Knowledge level | Method | Stated uncertainty |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 | PULS calculation | — |
| 1 | Simple mass balance | 135% |
| 2 | Uncalibrated 1D hydrodynamic model (Mike Urban) | 100% |
| 3 | Calibrated Mike Urban | 55% |
| 4 | Software sensor / CFD | 45% |
| 5 | Measurement-based | 30% |

PULS records 'antal overløb' - the number of overflows. It records no flow rate, no peak and no threshold crossing, so a high-flow event that scours accumulated basin sediment cannot be distinguished from a small dilute one.

That matters because sediment resuspension is a **threshold** in flow, not a frequency of events. Below the critical shear stress the deposit stays in the basin; above it, the accumulated sludge leaves — and the hazardous-substances programme reports the highest median metal concentrations precisely in basin sludge. Multiplying an annual volume by an average concentration cannot represent that, and the resulting error is one-directional and largest in the wettest years.

## 3. Nitrogen mass is the wrong currency

Oxygen depletion is the damage. Nitrogen is one route to it. Converting everything to oxygen demand — Redfield stoichiometry, which is chemistry rather than judgement:

| Pathway | Oxygen demand |
|---|---:|
| Remineralisation of algal biomass | **19.70 g O₂ per g N** |
| Remineralisation, via phosphorus | 142.6 g O₂ per g P |
| **Nitrification of delivered ammonium** | **4.57 g O₂ per g N** |
| **Sulphide oxidation** | **2.00 g O₂ per g S** |
| Methane oxidation | 3.99 g O₂ per g CH₄ |
| Direct BOD | 1.46 g O₂ per g BOD₅ |

Only the first line is modelled by the accounts. Three consequences:

**Ammonium carries its own demand.** An ammonium-rich discharge — which is what a basin that has gone anaerobic produces — consumes 4.57 g O₂ per g N on arrival, with no algae, no light and no delay. That pathway is absent from a nitrogen→algae→decay model entirely.

**Sulphate makes the debt effectively unbounded.** Seawater holds ~2700 mg/l sulphate = 901 mg/l as sulphur, against ~8 mg/l dissolved oxygen. Fully reduced and later re-oxidised that is 1799 mg/l of demand — **225× the oxygen in the water**. Reducing just 0.44% of the sulphate pool stores a debt equal to all of it. Once a basin goes anoxic the electron acceptor is effectively infinite, and every storm that mixes oxygen in spends it re-oxidising sulphide instead of restoring the water.

**And the ratio matters, not just the total.** Diatoms need silicon roughly 1:1 with nitrogen. Fertiliser and sewage add N and P; neither adds silicon, which comes from rock weathering. Enrichment therefore raises N:Si, and when silicon runs out the community shifts from diatoms to flagellates and dinoflagellates. A composition change driven by a ratio, invisible to any nitrogen total.

There is no potency term anywhere in the accounting. A kilogram delivered in February into a mixed column counts identically to a kilogram delivered in July into a stratified fjord — though vertical mixing across a summer pycnocline is roughly four orders of magnitude weaker, which is what decides whether the nitrogen is used or flushed.

## 4. What is not measured

| What | Window | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| iltsvind DCE | Jul-Aug, Aug-Sep, Sep-Oct, Oct-Nov | set by mechanism (the stratified season) |
| soft bottom fauna | 1 March - 31 May | set by programme design |
| bathing water | bathing season, roughly June-September | set by human use **(circular)** |
| fedtemoeg as a shore condition | no systematic national monitoring | set by when people are on beaches and complain **(circular)** |

The most consequential row is the second. **Soft-bottom fauna is sampled 1 March to 31 May** — after the winter, before the summer. The annual die-off is observed only in its aftermath, months later, once recolonisation has begun. So the event that decides which organisms are present to receive the next year's nitrogen is the one thing nobody watches.

And any seasonal claim drawn from a seasonally-sampled record is circular unless the window was set by mechanism. Iltsvind's July–November window was; bathing water's June–September window was not.

### Toxicants: eleven stations

The national typetal for hazardous substances in rain-dependent discharges — applied to all **19,665** discharge points — rest on **6 combined-sewer overflows and 5 separate stormwater outlets**, monitored 2000–2020.

The catchments were deliberately chosen to represent households and residential areas. The typetal cover only discharges *typetal cover only discharges 'uden forudgaaende rensning i form af fx filtrering eller sedimentation i regnvandsbassiner'*, and the report states they are limiting for *explicitly limiting for 'industriomraader og meget trafikerede veje'*.

Meanwhile the same programme reports the highest median metal concentrations in sludge from basins. **The design excludes the worst cases, and says so.**

| µg/l | Combined overflow | Separate stormwater | Max observed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Zink | 170 | 130 | 400 |
| Kobber | 16 | 9.0 | 50 |
| Bly | 5.0 | 4.0 | 24 |
| Chrom | 2.4 | 4.0 | 8.0 |
| Nikkel | 2.7 | 4.0 | 9.0 |
| Arsen | 0.8 | 1.3 | 4.0 |
| Cadmium | 0.1 | 0.07 | 0.31 |
| Kviksoelv | 0.05 | 0.03 | 0.3 |

Note that chromium, nickel and arsenic are **higher in separated stormwater than in sewage overflow** — and nationally there is 195 million m³/yr of the former against 33 million m³/yr of the latter. Separating a sewer system solves the sewage overflow and delivers untreated road runoff instead.

## 5. A worked case: Køge Bugt

DCE's oxygen reports state, in both 2023 and 2025: *"Køge Bugt og Faxe Bugt er der ikke registreret iltsvind i år"* — **no oxygen depletion registered**, including in 2023, the worst iltsvind year in two decades.

DCE's iltsvind is dissolved oxygen below 4 mg/l in bottom water, mapped in stratified basins. Køge Bugt is shallow and mixes readily, so it may never qualify while still having anoxic sediment, sulphate reduction and a putrefying shoreline. 'Not registered' is probably accurate and says nothing about whether the bay is dead.

## What this does and does not establish

**It does not establish that urban discharge is the dominant national source.** Under every reconstruction attempted, including ones stacked generously in that direction, agriculture remained the largest nitrogen term. Stacking every identified bias in the overflow figures reaches roughly 10% of the land-based term — material, far above the published 0.6%, and still not the driver.

**It does establish that the published percentage answers a different question than the one it is used for.** It answers: *of the nitrogen we observe arriving by stream from Danish land, how much do we attribute to farming after subtracting our models of everything else?* It is read as: *how much of Denmark's marine nitrogen problem is agriculture?*

Those differ by a closed set, a potency term, and a state variable. The accounts contain none of the three.

**Bounds marked as ours are ours.** Several ranges in section 1 are constructed here from the biases identified, not published figures. They are stated so they can be argued with.
