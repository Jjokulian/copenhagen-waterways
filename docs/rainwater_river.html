The rain, above ground

What section 2 of the argument would actually look like: the pipes hand the rain to a channel instead of to the sewer, the channel runs as a river through the city, and it ends in shallow reed cells on Amager that settle what the streets gave it. This is an artist's impression. The arrangement is the proposal; the shapes, the routes, the buildings and the birds are invented. Nothing here is a survey.

It exists to settle one confusion. This is not sewage. What runs in this channel is rain, the sediment the rain drags off the streets, and — in the scenario where the chemicals people put down drains are dealt with at source — the water from a shower or a washing machine. The toilet and the kitchen never enter it. They stay in a pipe that goes to a treatment works, which is the dark line at the edge of this scene and the only thing in it that smells. The argument →

      Why it can look like this

      A stormwater pond is not a sewage works because the two are removing different things. Sewage treatment breaks down dissolved and biological load, which needs aeration, energy and sludge handling. Street runoff carries its pollution attached to particles — metals, PAH, tyre wear, microplastics — and particles fall out of still water on their own. The machine is gravity, reeds and time.

      Which is also why the cells are shallow, wide and planted rather than deep and still, and why one of them is always out of service being dredged. A cell that is never dredged becomes the thing it was built to prevent — that is the condition attached to the whole idea, and it is drawn here rather than left in a maintenance plan.

      Held against the same standard as everything else here: this picture establishes nothing. It is what the proposal would look like if it worked, drawn so that “light treatment on Amager” stops meaning “sewage ponds in a bird reserve”, which it never did.
