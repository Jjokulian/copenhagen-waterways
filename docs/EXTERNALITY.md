# The externality expense economy

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-KIND">**This page is an argument, not a finding.** It proposes a fiscal instrument, works out what it would do to the products it applies to, and states what would make it wrong. Nothing in it is measured by this project, and it is kept out of the investigation pages for that reason.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-KIND "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-PART">It belongs to the same part of the project as [the programme](PROGRAMME.md): what ought to be done, labelled as such.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-PART "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-THREE">It is here because three separate arguments elsewhere in this project all end at the same missing piece.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-THREE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-A1">[Section 4](PROGRAMME.md) says a novo-chemical has to be **destroyed**, and destruction is a cost nobody is obliged to pay.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-A1 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-A2">[The industry question](PROGRAMME.md) says the demand for destruction is currently liability demand — courts, after the fact, in another country.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-A2 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
- <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-A3">[The REACH row](PROGRAMME.md) says a product ban is harmonised at EU level, so no city, region or utility can start one.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-A3 "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-LEVY">A levy is none of those things. It is a national fiscal instrument, it applies before the harm rather than after it, and it is the lever that stays available when market access is closed off.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-LEVY "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## The model, in five clauses

1. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CL1">**A product carries a levy at purchase, sized to its pollutive potential** — and covering only a fraction of it.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CL1 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
2. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CL2">**The buyer holds a return obligation:** hand the object back for destruction or recovery *before it deteriorates past the point where it can be collected*. Fabric before it is fibre, pellets before they are micro, micro before nano.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CL2 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
3. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CL3">**Return in time and the fraction was the whole price.** The levy behaves as a deposit, not a tax.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CL3 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
4. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CL4">**Fail to return and the buyer is billed the full externality, computed non-conservatively** — the upper estimate, not the cautious one.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CL4 "What this claim rests on")</sup>
5. <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CL5">**The supplier is fined for having sold to a buyer who did not return it.**</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CL5 "What this claim rests on")</sup>

### Why the shape matters more than the rate

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-AMBIG">**It prices ambiguity against whoever can resolve it.** Non-conservative billing inverts the usual burden. An unreturned object is assumed to have done its worst, and the only way to rebut that assumption is to produce the object — which is exactly what the person holding it can do and nobody else can.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-AMBIG "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-BAN">A ban or a liability claim, by contrast, needs a regulator or a court to show a harm that is diffuse by construction.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-BAN "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-DEPOSIT">**It is a deposit, so it is revenue-neutral for the compliant.** That changes the political argument it has to win. A tax is fought on whether the state should have the money. A deposit is fought on whether you intend to bring the thing back, which is a much worse position to defend from.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-DEPOSIT "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-DEMAND">**It supplies the demand curve that destruction capacity does not have.** The unmanaged price exceeds the managed price by construction, at a ratio the levy sets. That is the piece the industry argument is missing: not a subsidy, not a court award years later, but a price on the day.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-DEMAND "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-CONTRAST">**It generates the contrast this project keeps saying nobody created.** Levies are set by states, rates differ, municipalities can add return infrastructure or not — so places differ, on dates, in a recorded way. That is [the plurality argument](PLACES.md) arriving through the tax code instead of through a trial protocol.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-CONTRAST "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## Leasing is the wrapper, and it already exists

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-FINE">The supplier fine is the clause that does the structural work, because the cheapest way to avoid being fined for your buyer is **not to have one.** Keep title, rent the product, carry the return obligation yourself.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-FINE "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-LEASE">The instrument for that is a lease, and leasing is not a new legal form that has to be invented for this: it is ordinary contract law, in which the lessor stays the legal owner and the lessee obtains the right to use the asset, with liability, condition on return and remedies set in the contract.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-LEASE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-MANDATE">So the precedent is in the **instrument** and it is missing in the **mandate**. Nobody has to invent a legal vehicle to make a pollutive product returnable. What this page has not found is a rule that says *this kind of chemistry may only be placed on the market under a return-bearing title*. Denmark already runs a deposit system for drinks containers, EU law already makes producers finance the collection and treatment of electronics and batteries, and a lease already keeps title with the lessor. The gap is that none of them is keyed to what the substance does when it disperses.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-MANDATE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-PRIOR">That gap is where the design decision sits, and it is a small one to state and a large one to legislate: **the levy class should be keyed to the evolutionary prior**, the same taxonomy [section 5](PROGRAMME.md) uses for source control. A substance life has met before has a concentration below which return is not worth compelling. A substance with no prior that never degrades has no such level, and it is the case where non-conservative billing does all the work.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-PRIOR "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## Four classes, and what the model does to each

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-EXAMPLES">The examples are not decorative. They mark where the model works as written, where it degrades into something weaker, and where it stops being applicable — and a proposal that cannot say where it stops is not a proposal.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-EXAMPLES "What this claim rests on")</sup>

### A — Returnable and identifiable: the model works as written

| Product | Why it fits | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Artificial turf pitches** | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-TURF">[100](SOURCES.md#F-a8a007229c)–[120](SOURCES.md#F-66679106b8) t of rubber granulate as infill on an ordinary Danish football pitch, part of which leaves the pitch for the surroundings and has to be replaced</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-TURF "What this claim rests on")</sup>; <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-TURF-BUYER">one buyer, one contract, one supplier</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-TURF-BUYER "What this claim rests on")</sup> | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-TURF-EFFECT">The fine lands on a supplier who sold to a club with no containment plan. Pitches become leased systems with a contracted end of life</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-TURF-EFFECT "What this claim rests on")</sup> |
| **Agricultural film and silage wrap** | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-FILM-FIT">Farm-level buyer, dealer-level supplier, field-scale plastic on exactly the deterioration schedule the model is built around</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-FILM-FIT "What this claim rests on")</sup> | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-FILM-EFFECT">Dealers become collection points; thicker reusable covers and returnable silos beat single-use film on total cost</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-FILM-EFFECT "What this claim rests on")</sup> |
| **Batteries and electronics** | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-BATT-FIT">Already under producer responsibility</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-BATT-FIT "What this claim rests on")</sup> | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-BATT-EFFECT">Nothing structural — the mechanism exists, and what producers are made to finance is collection and treatment, not the externality. This is the precedent, not the frontier</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-BATT-EFFECT "What this claim rests on")</sup> |
| **Nets, ropes, dolly rope** | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-NETS-FIT">Ghost gear *is* the unreturned item</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-NETS-FIT "What this claim rests on")</sup> | <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-NETS-EFFECT">Gear becomes an asset on a register rather than a consumable</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-NETS-EFFECT "What this claim rests on")</sup> |

### B — Disperses during use: return cannot catch it

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-TYRES">**Tyres** are the clean case. The worn tyre is an object that can be handed back; the wear left on the road while driving is the externality, and no return obligation reaches it. So the levy cannot be a deposit here — it has to be sized to the **wear fraction** and hypothecated to capture: ponds, gully traps, street sweeping. Which is this project's [pond argument](PROGRAMME.md) arriving from the fiscal side, and it is the same money.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-TYRES "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-ABRASION">The supply effect is not on the return channel but on the product: **abrasion becomes a priced attribute**.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-ABRASION "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-TYRELABEL">The EU tyre label already grades rolling resistance and wet grip, and the labelling regulation empowers the Commission to add abrasion once reliable test methods exist.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-TYRELABEL "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-SAMECLASS">Brake pads, antifouling paint and ski wax sit in the same class, and for them the honest reduction is a use restriction plus a hypothecated levy rather than a deposit.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-SAMECLASS "What this claim rests on")</sup>

### C — Atomised consumables: identification fails

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-ATOMISED">Cigarette filters, wipes, glitter, sachets. There is nothing to return and nobody to bill, so the model collapses to a plain levy plus the supplier fine — a weaker instrument, and worth admitting as one. The only real lever left is **format**: refill, concentrate, dispenser, deposit-bearing container. The accountable party becomes the retailer, because the retailer is the last identifiable person in the chain.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-ATOMISED "What this claim rests on")</sup>

### D — A novo-chemical inside a returnable product: the interesting case

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-PFAS">A PFAS jacket comes back. The PFAS left during use and does not degrade.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-PFAS "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-RESERVED">Non-conservative billing on that residue is effectively prohibitive — which means **the model delivers reserved use by price rather than by ban.** That is the outcome [section 5](PROGRAMME.md) argues for and [the REACH row](PROGRAMME.md) says no city may impose.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-RESERVED "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-DECLARE">And it has a second effect that is better than the first. A supplier who declares a product free of the substance pays no levy — and is liable if the declaration is wrong. **That converts a marketing claim into a fiscal declaration**, dated and auditable the way a tax return is.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-DECLARE "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## What it does to the supply landscape

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-SUPPLY-INTRO">This is the part worth thinking through, because a levy that only changed prices would be a rounding error. What it changes is what kinds of firms can operate.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-SUPPLY-INTRO "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-SERVICES">**Products become services wherever return matters.** Not as an aspiration — as the cheapest available defence against the supplier fine. Pitch systems, film, workwear, protective clothing, equipment: the firms that keep title stop being exposed to their customers' behaviour. Ownership becomes the expensive way to sell a dispersing product.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-SERVICES "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-MONO">**Mono-material and repairable design wins on cost, not on virtue.** A returned object has recovery value only if it can be taken apart; a blended textile and a bonded assembly are worth less at return than they cost to process. The levy makes that difference visible at the point of design instead of at the end of life.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-MONO "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-VARIETY">**Variety collapses where return channels are expensive, and that is a real cost.** Every product variant needs its own return path, so the model penalises catalogue proliferation. Some of that is pure gain — fewer, longer-lived, better-specified products. Some of it is consolidation: a small supplier cannot carry unbounded liability alone, so **without a pooled compliance scheme this instrument concentrates the market**, which is an objection the design has to answer rather than an incidental effect.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-VARIETY "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-INSURANCE">**Insurance enters, and becomes the fast regulator.** Unbounded liability is exactly what an insurance market prices, and an insurer that underwrites return-failure will price chemistry directly: cheaper premiums for substances that degrade, punitive ones for those that do not. **That is a private regulator that can reprice at each renewal rather than wait the years a REACH restriction takes** — and it arrives through a member state's tax code rather than through the harmonised market, which is the whole point of choosing a fiscal instrument.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-INSURANCE "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-REACHYEARS">On the European Environmental Bureau's count, in the few years before the EU's 2020 chemicals strategy authorities took [5](SOURCES.md#F-76399c5b53) years and seven months on average to adopt a REACH restriction.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-REACHYEARS "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-SECONDHAND">**Second-hand markets formalise instead of dying.** The obligation transfers with title, deposit and all, which turns resale into a recorded change of custody. Durable goods gain; the disposal-by-resale route closes.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-SECONDHAND "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-IMPORTS">**Imports are reachable.** A levy falls on imported goods as on domestic ones: an importer posts the liability or does not place the goods.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-IMPORTS "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-FASHION">**And fast fashion breaks first**, which is worth stating plainly rather than leaving as an implication. Its unit economics depend on garments not coming back. A model that prices non-return removes the assumption the business rests on — and the firms that already run take-back, rental and repair are advantaged by an instrument they are already paying for voluntarily.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-FASHION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

## What would make this wrong

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-VALUATION">**The valuation is the whole instrument, and it is contestable.** "Non-conservative externality" is a number somebody has to compute, and by this project's own standard a number nobody can recompute is not a number. The method has to be published, versioned, and applied to the substance rather than to the industry. Without that, the model is an invitation to arbitrary billing and would deserve to lose in court.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-VALUATION "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-RUIN">**Ruin and regressivity.** A household billed the full non-conservative externality of a sofa is a headline, not a policy. Three answers exist — a cap, mandatory insurance, or return infrastructure so easy that failure is a genuine choice — and only the third leaves the mechanism intact, because the other two re-cap the thing the model exists to uncap. That makes convenient return a **precondition** of the instrument rather than an accompaniment to it.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-RUIN "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-LAUNDER">**Laundering routes.** Resale without transfer, "lost", "stolen", export. The supplier fine is what makes designing against these the supplier's problem, but each needs a written rule.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-LAUNDER "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-EXPORT">Deposit systems already meet the export route: German supermarkets near the Danish border exempt Scandinavian residents from the deposit if they sign an export declaration.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-EXPORT "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-HAZARD">**The mirror of the moral hazard in the industry argument.** An industry paid to receive returns acquires an interest in returns being made, which is benign; an industry paid out of failure-to-return acquires an interest in failure, which is not. The revenue from the fourth clause must not fund the party that administers the second clause.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-HAZARD "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-DRS">Denmark's own deposit operator is funded in part by the deposits on bottles that are not returned,</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-DRS "What this claim rests on")</sup> <span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-DRS-POINT">so the Danish system already carries the incentive this rule is meant to prevent.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-DRS-POINT "What this claim rests on")</sup>

<span class="claim" data-claim="C-LE-E-NOTCOSTED">**And the honest one: this has not been costed.** No levy rate is proposed here, no elasticity is estimated, and no Danish legal opinion has been taken on whether the fifth clause survives contact with proportionality. The argument is that the *shape* is right — a deposit with an uncapped tail, keyed to what a substance does when it disperses, wrapped in a lease. The rate, the register and the law are work that has not been done.</span><sup class="claim-mark">[†](CLAIMS.md#C-LE-E-NOTCOSTED "What this claim rests on")</sup>
