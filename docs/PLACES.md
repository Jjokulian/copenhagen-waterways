# Places, not categories

The rest of this project asks whether a category is real. This page does the opposite:
it starts from **named places where somebody says something is wrong**, asks what is
measured there, and asks what would be enough to fix it.

Sometimes that needs a cause. Often it does not — and that matters here, because
[40 of 166 mechanisms are unscoreable](hypodrafts/TRIAGE.md) and may stay that way. **A
solution that works whichever mechanism dominates does not have to wait for a triage
that may never finish.**

## Mariager Fjord — the worst measured, and nitrogen does not reach it

**Claimed:** a fjord that died. It is the site of Denmark's most notorious fish kill.

**Measured:** 35.1% of near-bed observations below 2 mg/l, median 5.09. Four times the
next region. And the archive's entire hydrogen-sulphide record — **2,598 of 2,601 rows**
— comes from a single station in this fjord, "Dybet", Mariager Fjord midterløb. Free
sulphide in the water column is what remains after the sediment's iron buffer is spent.

**What is known about cause, and it is unusually definite.** Windolf et al. (2012)
studied ten catchments over twenty years. Eight responded to reductions in agricultural
nitrogen surplus within five years. **Mariager showed no response at all** — high diffuse
loads throughout, and the highest intercept in the study. The authors attribute it to old,
oxic groundwater in chalk aquifers, where nitrate survives instead of being denitrified.
The fjord is also a deep silled basin, so its bottom water exchanges rarely.

**So the honest statement is that the two candidate explanations are both physical**: a
sill that prevents exchange, and an aquifer that delays whatever the land does by
decades. Neither is addressed by a nitrogen target, and the paper's own data says the
nitrogen lever was pulled and nothing moved.

**What would be sufficient**

*Cause-agnostic — works whichever mechanism dominates:* deep-water exchange. If bottom
water is anoxic because it does not turn over, moving it is a direct fix and does not
require knowing why it became anoxic. This is engineering with precedent: forced
downwelling and artificial deep-water oxygenation have been run at basin scale in
Sweden's Byfjorden and trialled in the Baltic. Cost and side effects are real and are
not assessed here.

*Cause-contingent:* nitrogen reduction in the catchment. **The evidence available says
this has already been tried at scale and produced no measurable response in this fjord
within twenty years.** That is not an argument against it elsewhere.

## Lillebælt and Det Sydfynske Øhav — the second tier, and the ones with a case

**Measured:** 9.2% and 6.2% below 2 mg/l, over 10,885 and 5,138 observations. Not
Mariager's severity, but an order of magnitude above the national pattern, and on the
largest station counts in the table.

These are stratified, sheltered waters with substantial catchments — which is the
combination the nutrient hypothesis actually predicts. **If the standard account is
right anywhere, it is here**, and this is where a nitrogen intervention should be tested
rather than assumed. That is a testable proposition and it is not tested in this
project.

## Køge Bugt — the loudest claim, and the measurement disagrees about what is wrong

**Claimed:** greasy water, a shore people would rather not walk on, fish kills. The most
publicly contested coastal water in Denmark, and the one nearest the largest population.

**Measured:** **0.1%** of near-bed observations below 2 mg/l. Median 10.30, the highest
in the table alongside Ringkøbing. DCE report no registered iltsvind here in 2023 or
2025 — including 2023, the worst iltsvind year in two decades.

**These are not in conflict.** They are answers to different questions. The bay is
shallow and mixes readily, so it may never meet a criterion defined as *oxygen below
4 mg/l in stratified bottom water* while still having anoxic sediment, sulphate
reduction and a fouled shoreline. **The measurement says the bay is not suffocating. It
says nothing about whether it is dead.**

Three of the four words the public uses have no instrument behind them at all —
*fedtemøg* has no measurement, *fiskedød* has no open register, and *livet i fjordene*
is a claim about structure that oxygen does not address. So for this place:

**What would be sufficient**

*Nothing that targets oxygen*, because oxygen is not the measured problem here. A
nitrogen reduction sized on a national residual will not change a shoreline that is
already at 10.3 mg/l.

*Cause-agnostic and available now:* the interventions in
[PROGRAMME.md](#PROGRAMME.md) that act on what arrives rather than on what it causes —
keeping rainwater out of the combined system, an outlet that is not the bay, and source
control on what the water carries. These change the delivered load whatever the
mechanism downstream, and their case does not rest on the nitrogen chain.

*And one measurement, which costs almost nothing:* **there is no instrument for the
thing people are actually complaining about.** Before proposing a fix for *fedtemøg*,
somebody has to be able to say whether it got better. That is the cheapest unblocking
step on this coast and it is not a modelling problem.

## What this page is for

Three things follow from reading by place rather than by mechanism.

1. **The severity ranking and the attention ranking are different.** Mariager is four
   times worse than anywhere and is not the subject of a national argument. Køge Bugt is
   among the best-oxygenated waters in the country and is the subject of one.
2. **The place where the standard account is most likely right is not the place it is
   most loudly applied.** Lillebælt and the Sydfynske Øhav fit the nutrient story;
   Mariager demonstrably does not, on twenty years of data.
3. **A cause-agnostic intervention is worth more than its evidence base suggests**,
   because the evidence base for *any* mechanism here is thin. Deep-water exchange does
   not care why the water is anoxic. Keeping sewage out of a bay does not care which of
   166 mechanisms is dominant. **Those are worth doing under uncertainty in a way that a
   precisely-targeted intervention is not.**

**Limits.** The table aggregates into official water-body names, which this project's
own testing says are not real units — it locates observations, it does not characterise
places. Coverage is uneven: 10 stations at Odense against 170 in Smålandsfarvandet.
And the claims side is not systematic; it comes from the public record rather than a
survey, so a place with a real problem and no local press does not appear here.
