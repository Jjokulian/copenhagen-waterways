Dial the density, choose what each node carries, and watch what it costs. Two populations, because they are different machines: inland nodes on the streams that deliver, and marine nodes in the fjords, belts and coastal waters that receive. The scatter is random — it is a picture of a density, not a siting plan — and the marine one is drawn inside the 123 water bodies the state actually recognises, all 43,579 km² of them.

Every default price here has no source: they came from a shopping list on SENSING that was retired because none of its prices had one. Each is a slider so that somebody can replace it with a quotation; until then the totals are illustrations, not estimates. The protocol these nodes would serve is Fingerprinting Denmark.

Bottles are how the cheap continuous sensors get calibrated at a site, not how it is monitored forever. Once the local relation between fluorescence, turbidity and the laboratory panel is established, the sensors carry the estimate and the bottles drop to validation. If that relation never stabilises, the site keeps sampling — and that is a finding too.
