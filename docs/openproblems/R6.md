# R6 — Sulphide locks the iron that would hold the phosphate

**Missing: experiment, constructive.** R6's discriminator is a *rate* against a *ratio* —
"Fe:S and Fe:P against phosphate release rate" — and its causal clause is comparative:
marine sediment releases more P under anoxia *because of the sulphate*. Nothing in Denmark
varies sulphate while holding organic loading, iron supply and temperature fixed, so the
contrast has to be built. A core incubation is the answer, and it is cheap.

## No water-column observable can reach it, and this should be said plainly

R6 lives in the millimetre above and below the sulphide front. The archive's finest
observable is a station-month median of near-bed oxygen: `stations_series.json`, verified
here — **679,422 station-months** (9 variables summing 60,460 … 84,668), 1,415 stations,
564 monthly slots from 1980; near-bed oxygen is **76,980**. That is four orders of
magnitude coarser than the front it would have to resolve, and the wrong quantity
besides: oxygen is R6's *precondition*, not its consequence. A correlation between bed
oxygen and water-column DIP is equally predicted by A7, R2, R5 and plain mineralisation.
**Class 6, absent dimension — unscoreable, not refuted.**

**The `Dihydrogensulfid` series, checked rather than assumed.** Two full independent scans
of `ctd.csv.gz` both return **2,601 rows**, and they are not a Danish series. **2,598 come
from one station** — `93610032` "Dybet", Mariager Fjord midterdel — with 1 row each from
three other Mariager sites; **210 casts on 207 dates**, 2001–2008 and 2010, nothing since;
µg/l; depths 2–66 m; 432 zeros; median 27.9, max 30,022 µg/l. Gear is `Ketcher` on every
row (a bottle, not a sonde; 1,941 rows carry `999 - Ukendt` as instrument). One
permanently stratified silled basin, one authority, nine years: usable for the H2S
climatology of Mariager Fjord's monolimnion and nothing comparative. It is also the wrong
side of the mechanism — **free sulphide in the water column is what remains after the
sediment's iron buffer is already spent**, a detector of the endpoint, not of the process.
ODA `vandkemi` carries `Svovlbrinte (sulfid)` at **1,125 historical stations**: a far
better series, measuring the same endpoint.

## The finding that changes R6's status

**Denmark had a written national method for R6's own variables and stopped running it.**
TA kap. 14 (1998) specifies the sulphide front, the sulphide **buffer capacity** — sediment
added to an H2S solution of known concentration, uptake measured after a fixed incubation,
reported as µmol H2S cm⁻², i.e. a *functional assay of the reactive iron pool* — and the
oxidised iron content in the buffer zone. SR323 reports results: front 51 mm spring → 25 mm
autumn; buffer capacity 29 → 15 µmol cm⁻²; oxidised Fe in the top 1.5 cm ranging 2 to 2,741
µmol, geometric mean 9.3 → 2.0 once Limfjorden and Ringkøbing are set aside. That is iron
consumed as the sulphide front advances, in Danish sediment, seasonally. The register's
"no Fe speciation for any Danish site" is wrong for 1998–2003 and right for everything
after. Today's ODA sediment topic has six parameters and none is iron.

Two error classes travel with it. **Class 2, censoring:** the buffer assay saturates at
≈100 µmol and is then reported as `>x µmol cm⁻²` — right-censored precisely at the
high-iron stations R6 cares most about. **Class 4, conflation** in DOME: one physical
sample appears twice as `SED63` and `SED2000`, two grain-size fractions with different
values; pooling them shifts any ratio. Values like `16400000.000000002 ug/kg` betray a
unit conversion inside DOME — that precision is manufactured.

## Why experiment, and the null it must be run against

Restoring the 1998 assay would give Fe and S as *state variables* — worth doing, and only a
measurement problem. It still would not test R6, which asserts a **causal dependence of P
release on sulphate**, and the field never varies sulphate alone.

**The null, computed under the constraint imposed, not quoted.** The naive null — "anoxic
sediment releases no more P than oxic sediment" — is wrong, and would hand R6 a win it has
not earned: anoxia releases P by organic mineralisation whether or not sulphide forms. The
constraint an incubation imposes is *same sediment, same anoxia, sulphate varied*. So the
null is **equality of P release per unit mineralisation across sulphate levels** — flux(PO₄)
normalised to concurrent flux(DIC) or NH₄ from the same core, sulphate the only manipulated
term. Under H₀ that ratio is flat in sulphate; R6 predicts it rises and saturates where
reactive Fe is exhausted. A design without a sulphate-free *anoxic* arm cannot reject
anything, however many replicates it has.

## Smallest unblocking thing

1. **Reinstate the 1998 assay at 20 existing `Bundfauna` stations, one spring and one autumn.**
   Silver-plate front, buffer capacity, oxidised Fe, alongside the Fe-adsorbed P already in
   `Sedimentkemi`. Report the censoring threshold with every `>` value. Cost: a protocol that
   already exists in Danish, a syringe and a photometer.
2. **The incubation.** One Danish sediment, homogenised, split across a sulphate dose series
   ~1 to ~28 mM at fixed ionic strength by NaCl substitution, so *sulphate* is the treatment.
   Track PO₄, DIC/NH₄, H2S and sulphate reduction rate in the overlying water; sequential Fe
   (ascorbate/dithionite) and S (AVS/CRS) on the cores at the end. PANGAEA 997075 makes every
   one of those measurements together, in the western Baltic, in 2025 — current, standard and
   adjacent. Its one omission is phosphorus, which is the whole of R6.
3. **Reclassification test, stated in advance.** If the NOVA-era rows (front, buffer, oxidised
   Fe, 1998–2003) are recoverable from the former county archives at ≥10 stations with ≥4
   paired seasons *and* a co-located Fe-adsorbed P value, R6's **state** half becomes
   **armchair** and this file is half wrong. The **rate** half stays experimental regardless.

## What it would and would not license

A successful incubation licenses one narrow thing: *in this sediment, sulphate availability
changes P release per unit mineralisation by this factor*. It would **not** license a Danish
phosphorus budget, a water-body allocation, or a claim about the Baltic feedback — one
sediment is one sediment, and R6's unit is depositional history, not a map. Nor the
freshwater comparison R6 makes in words: that needs the same dose series on lake sediment,
and Danish lake and marine sediments differ in far more than sulphate. The honest limit is
that it tests the *step* — sulphide strips Fe, Fe was holding P — not the *system claim*
that this is what makes the marine case worse.
