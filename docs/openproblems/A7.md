# A7 — Sediment nutrient regeneration

**Missing: armchair for the weak form, measurement for the strong form.**

A7 says the bed releases stored N and P back into the water in summer. Its two
discriminators fall in different categories:

- **the DIP seasonality ratio against the sediment organic pool** — both sides exist in
  ODA, neither is fetched, the join is small. **Armchair.** Tentative, not an obstacle.
- **benthic flux chambers** — for Danish inner waters this is close to unrecorded. I
  went looking specifically and counted what exists; see below. **Measurement.**

Nothing in this repository carries a nutrient concentration, so A7 cannot be scored
today in any form. That is a fetch away, not a refutation.

## Job 1 — sources found

### The best one, verified by download and parse

**Hylén et al. (2025), *In situ-measured benthic fluxes of dissolved inorganic
phosphorus in the Baltic Sea***
· data: `https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14812160` (concept) → record **17465937**,
one file `BalticSeaInSituFluxDIP_v3.xlsx`, **852,169 bytes**, **CC-BY-4.0**
· paper: `https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6423/2025/`

I downloaded and parsed it. Sheet `StationOverview` holds **498 flux rows**, **59
stations**, **2001–2021**, one row per chamber unit per deployment, carrying DIP flux
with its standard error, bottom-water O₂, salinity, temperature, depth, sediment type,
sediment organic carbon, an oxygen class, lander type, lat/lon, year and month.
**Per-measurement, not a summary** — a directly measured benthic release rate, which is
the rarest object in the whole A7 problem.

**And it is almost entirely outside Denmark.** By subbasin: EGB 237, NBP 82, WGB 65,
Gulf of Finland 60, Gulf of Bothnia 30, **Bornholm Basin 18, Arkona 6**. Only **5 of 59
stations lie west of 15.5°E** — BY2 (14.099°E, 54.975°N), HB3, HB1, HB2, BY5, all around
Bornholm. The paper states outright that it *"does not consider the Kattegat and the Belt
Sea … as being part of the Baltic Sea."* **Zero stations in the Kattegat, the Belts, the
Sound, or any Danish fjord.** Months run 2–11 — no December or January anywhere in it.
Sediment types: mud/muddy sand 372, mixed 113, coarse 11, sand 2.

**Independence: high.** A chamber lander incubating a patch of bed is a different
instrument, a different quantity and different operators (Gothenburg/GEOMAR, not Danish
monitoring) from the CTD sonde. If usable it is a *fourth* production path beside CTD,
ocean colour and Secchi.

**That asymmetry is the whole problem.** The response side is ~1,125 stations; the
sediment pool side is 39 stations in the entire national record.

**An unresolved discrepancy, flagged not settled.** `NOVANA-PROG` (Miljøstyrelsen,
2023–2027) states sediment nutrient pools are taken at 16–19 stations a year *"across 89
of 109 water bodies"*, at 7 depths, **every January–February**. ODA exposes 39 stations
ever. Either that rotation has not run or ODA does not expose the whole record. Ask
before quoting either number.

## Does this break the class-7 residual, or inherit it?

The parent charge is fair: internal loading is normally *inferred* as what is left over
after a mass balance, which makes it model-as-datum by construction. FR483 is the local
instance — a model of Aarhus Bay whose sediment term is fitted, not read.

- The **DIP-seasonality form breaks it.** It sets one directly observed quantity
  (summer minus winter ortho-phosphate near the bed) against another (loss-on-ignition
  and Fe-adsorbed P in the bed at the same position). No budget is closed, nothing is a
  remainder. The price is that it is **weak**: a summer DIP rise over organic-rich bed is
  equally consistent with water-column regeneration co-located with organic-rich bed,
  since deposition drives both. It can *falsify* the benthic signature — no covariance
  means A7 leaves no mark at station resolution — but a positive result does not isolate
  the bed.
- The **chamber form breaks it completely**, is the only thing that isolates the bed, and
  Denmark has essentially none of it.
- **The Jan–Feb sampling is why the residual keeps reappearing.** The pool is measured
  once a year, in winter; the release is claimed for summer. With one annual pool
  observation the summer drawdown *cannot* be a difference of two measurements, so
  anyone who wants a number has to infer it. The programme design, not anyone's laziness,
  is what forces class 7 here.

## Smallest unblocking thing

1. **One fetch, in this order.** `Emne_12_19` (Sedimentkemi) first — 39 stations ever,
   trivially small, and it decides how many positions the test can have. Then
   `Emne_10_11` (Vandkemi), tens of MB. `scripts/fetch_oda.py` implements neither:
   `TOPICS` has five keys and `argparse(choices=...)` rejects both, so this is one dict
   entry each, with the node ids already recorded in `data/manual/data_sources.json`.
   Set an explicit period — the criterion pane is period-filtered and silently returns
   the current network otherwise.
2. **Then check the join before designing anything.** Count stations that carry *both* a
   sediment core and ≥5 years of monthly DIP within, say, 2 km. If that count is in single
   digits the armchair classification is wrong and A7 is a measurement gap in both forms.
   Stating this in advance is the point.
3. **The measurement, and it is cheap.** Sample the existing sediment stations **twice** —
   late February and late September — instead of once in winter. That single change turns
   the summer release from a residual into a difference of two measured pools, at
   whatever station count the programme already funds. Second priority: one chamber lander
   deployment in the Kattegat or a Danish fjord, anywhere, since the count there is zero.

## What a completed test would and would not license

**Would.** Whether near-bed phosphate seasonality covaries with bed organic content
across positions, as a coefficient with its qualifiers — and, using the Zenodo file, a
check of whether the CTD-derived bottom-oxygen field agrees with the independently
measured bottom-water O₂ recorded beside each chamber flux. That check is available now
for the 5 stations near Bornholm and is worth doing on its own.

**Would not.** Any share, tonnage or "internal loading accounts for n%": that is a mass
balance and this is not one. Nor anything about Danish inner waters from the Zenodo data
— those 498 fluxes describe basins the paper defines as excluding the Kattegat and the
Belt Sea, and carrying them westward would be the aggregation error this project audits
elsewhere.
