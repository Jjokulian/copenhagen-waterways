# A7 — Sediment nutrient regeneration

**Missing: armchair for the weak form, measurement for the strong form.**

A7 says the bed releases stored N and P in summer. Its two discriminators fall in
different categories:

- **the DIP seasonality ratio against the sediment organic pool** — both sides exist in
  ODA, neither is fetched, the join is small. **Armchair.** Tentative, not an obstacle.
- **benthic flux chambers** — for Danish inner waters, close to unrecorded. I counted
  what exists. **Measurement.**

Nothing in this repository carries a nutrient concentration, so A7 cannot be scored today
in any form. That is a fetch away, not a refutation.

## Job 1 — sources found

### The best one, verified by download and parse

**Hylén et al. (2025), *In situ-measured benthic fluxes of dissolved inorganic
phosphorus in the Baltic Sea*** — `https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14812160` (concept) →
record **17465937**, one file `BalticSeaInSituFluxDIP_v3.xlsx`, **852,169 bytes**,
**CC-BY-4.0**; paper `https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6423/2025/`.

I downloaded and parsed it. Sheet `StationOverview` holds **498 flux rows**, **59
stations**, **2001–2021**, one row per chamber unit per deployment, carrying DIP flux
with its standard error, bottom-water O₂, salinity, temperature, depth, sediment type,
sediment organic carbon, oxygen class, lander type, lat/lon, year and month.
**Per-measurement, not a summary** — a directly measured benthic release rate, the rarest
object in the whole A7 problem.

**And it is almost entirely outside Denmark.** By subbasin: EGB 237, NBP 82, WGB 65,
Gulf of Finland 60, Gulf of Bothnia 30, **Bornholm Basin 18, Arkona 6**. Only **5 of 59
stations lie west of 15.5°E** — BY2 (14.099°E, 54.975°N), HB3, HB1, HB2, BY5, all near
Bornholm. The paper states it *"does not consider the Kattegat and the Belt Sea … as
being part of the Baltic Sea."* **Zero stations in the Kattegat, the Belts, the Sound or
any Danish fjord.** Months run 2–11 — no December or January anywhere. Sediment types:
mud/muddy sand 372, mixed 113, coarse 11, sand 2.

**Independence: high.** A chamber lander incubating a patch of bed is a different
instrument, quantity and operator (Gothenburg/GEOMAR, not Danish monitoring) from the CTD
sonde — a *fourth* production path beside CTD, ocean colour and Secchi.

is 39 in the entire national record.

**An unresolved discrepancy, flagged not settled.** `NOVANA-PROG` (Miljøstyrelsen,
2023–2027) states sediment nutrient pools are taken at 16–19 stations a year *"across 89
of 109 water bodies"*, at 7 depths, **every January–February**. ODA exposes 39 ever.
Either that rotation has not run or ODA does not expose the whole record. Ask before
quoting either number.

## Does this break the class-7 residual, or inherit it?

Internal loading is normally *inferred* as what is left over after a mass balance, which
makes it model-as-datum by construction. FR483 is the local instance: a model of Aarhus
Bay whose sediment term is fitted, not read.

- The **DIP-seasonality form breaks it.** It sets one directly observed quantity
  (summer minus winter ortho-phosphate near the bed) against another (loss-on-ignition
  and Fe-adsorbed P at the same position). No budget is closed, nothing is a remainder.
  The price is that it is **weak**: a summer DIP rise over organic-rich bed is equally
  consistent with water-column regeneration co-located with it, since deposition drives
  both. It can *falsify* the benthic signature — no covariance means A7 leaves no mark at
  station resolution — but a positive result does not isolate the bed.
- The **chamber form breaks it completely**, is the only thing that isolates the bed, and
  Denmark has essentially none of it.
- **The Jan–Feb sampling is why the residual keeps reappearing.** The pool is measured
  once a year, in winter; the release is claimed for summer. With one annual observation
  the summer drawdown *cannot* be a difference of two measurements, so anyone wanting a
  number must infer it. The programme design forces class 7 here.

## Smallest unblocking thing

1. **One fetch, in this order.** `Emne_12_19` (Sedimentkemi) first — 39 stations ever,
   trivially small, and it fixes how many positions the test can have. Then `Emne_10_11`
   (Vandkemi), tens of MB. `scripts/fetch_oda.py` implements neither: `TOPICS` has five
   keys and `argparse(choices=...)` rejects both, so it is one dict entry each, node ids
   already recorded in `data/manual/data_sources.json`. Set an explicit period — the
   criterion pane is period-filtered and silently returns the current network otherwise.
2. **Check the join before designing anything.** Count stations carrying *both* a
   sediment core and ≥5 years of monthly DIP within 2 km. If that is in single digits the
   armchair classification is wrong and A7 is a measurement gap in both forms. Stating
   this in advance is the point.
3. **The measurement, and it is cheap.** Sample the existing sediment stations **twice**,
   late February and late September, instead of once in winter. That one change turns the
   summer release from a residual into a difference of two measured pools, at whatever
   station count is already funded. Then: one chamber deployment anywhere in the Kattegat,
   the Belts or a Danish fjord, since the count there is zero.

## What a completed test would and would not license

**Would.** Whether near-bed phosphate seasonality covaries with bed organic content
across positions, as a coefficient with its qualifiers. Separately, the Zenodo file makes
one check possible now: whether the CTD-derived bottom-oxygen field agrees with the
independently measured bottom-water O₂ recorded beside each chamber flux, at the 5
stations near Bornholm.

**Would not.** Any share, tonnage or "internal loading accounts for n%": that is a mass
balance and this is not one. Nor anything about Danish inner waters from the Zenodo data,
whose basins the paper defines as excluding the Kattegat and the Belt Sea — carrying them
westward would be the aggregation error this project audits elsewhere.
