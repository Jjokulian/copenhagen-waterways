# J1 — Transparent exopolymer particles and marine gel

**Missing: measurement** — specifically the second kind, *a new metric over what is
already recorded*. The metric can be written down. It cannot be attributed. Both halves
of that are the finding.

## The direct quantity is absent, and this is now checked rather than assumed

`J1` says gel production rises when N:P is skewed **in either direction**, and it names
its own discriminator: TEP concentration against ambient N:P and bloom senescence stage,
not against nutrient load.

Enumerating the `Parameter` column of the whole CTD extract (`data/raw/oda/ctd.csv.gz`,
one streaming pass) returns **17 distinct parameters** and no more:

> Temperatur 7,501,201 · Salinitet 7,474,425 · Oxygen indhold 7,146,792 · **Fluorescens
> 6,783,570** · Oxygenmætning 6,598,099 · **Lysdæmpning 5,217,330** · Konduktivitet
> 5,169,272 · Vægtfylde 4,329,607 · **Turbiditet 1,076,915** · **FDOM 890,921** ·
> Photometer måling 576,179 · Photometer reference 531,071 · pH 237,445 ·
> Strømhastighed 84,734 · Strømretning 84,572 · Farvestof 4,601 · Dihydrogensulfid 2,601

No TEP, no polysaccharide, no Alcian blue, no gel, no microlayer. This confirms
`J1-J2-J3-GAP` in `DATA_QUEUE.md` from the file rather than from the note. In-water N and
P concentrations are also not in the local raw set — `data/raw/oda/stations.csv` is a
station/measuring-point registry, and `ODA-TILFOERSEL` (queued, unfetched) is
*region-aggregated load*, which is the exact quantity `J1` says not to use. So the
hypothesis is missing both its dependent variable and its discriminator.

**Absent dimension: `J1` is unscoreable, not refuted.**

## The new metric, stated precisely enough to be rejected

The interesting part is that the archive turns out to hold more optics than the derived
cube exposes. `docs/data/areas/stations_series.json` carries 679,422 station-months over
9 variables, of which only one is optical (`fluo`, 60,460 station-months, unit `rel`).
The raw CTD file carries three more, and they co-occur.

Counting distinct (station, date) casts by which of the four optical sensors fired:

| sensors present on the cast | casts |
|---|---:|
| fluorescence + attenuation | 74,391 |
| fluorescence + attenuation + turbidity | 2,148 |
| fluorescence + attenuation + FDOM | 191 |
| **all four (fluo + FDOM + attenuation + turbidity)** | **9,717** |
| any FDOM at all | 10,589 (211 stations, **2021–2026 only**) |

That last row is the whole opportunity and the whole limit. FDOM — fluorescent dissolved
organic matter, the CDOM proxy — begins in 2021.

The metric would be, per cast in the 9,717:

> **R = Lysdæmpning − f(Fluorescens, FDOM, Turbiditet)**

attenuation not explained by the chlorophyll, the coloured dissolved matter and the
mineral particles that are supposed to explain it. If marine gel has any optical
expression, this is where it would sit. `RESIDUAL.md` gives the only test such a number
admits: **R must shrink by exactly P when a part P is measured out of it.**

## Why it does not identify J1 — three reasons, computed here

**1. The residual is smaller than the disagreement between two measurements of the
total.** On the 6,879 same-day ship/satellite pairs in `data/derived/matchups.json`, the
log ratio of satellite Kd490 to ship Kd(PAR) has **sd 0.352** (median ratio 0.769, p10–p90
0.566–1.188). Regressing log KD490 on log CHL in two single-day slices of
`data/raw/cmems/grid/{transp,plankton}__inner__2015.nc` (n = 22,916 and 15,624) leaves a
residual of **sd 0.172 and 0.206** in log units. The candidate signal is roughly *half*
the error bar on the total it is subtracted from. A satellite-only version of this metric
is dead on arrival, and the reason is arithmetic, not judgement.

**2. The shrink-by-P test is vacuous here, because P is fitted rather than measured.**
Adding FDOM to the regression does reduce R. But FDOM's *contribution to attenuation* is
the fitted coefficient of the same regression that produces R — it is not an independent
measurement of that contribution. The account is circular in exactly the way
`RESIDUAL.md` describes, and the prediction `R_new = R_old − P` has no independent `P` to
check against. It would become a real test only if the CDOM absorption coefficient were
measured, which it is not: FDOM is a relative fluorescence, and `fluo` is published with
unit `rel`.

**3. The total is not a property of the water.** `LIGHT.md` establishes that broadband
Kd falls with depth in uniform water because the surviving spectrum shifts; casts
beginning above 2 m give median Kd 0.35 and those beginning below 2 m give 0.27 — a 23%
swing produced by an undocumented metadata choice. Error class **7, model-as-datum**, on
the input the residual is defined against. (The CTD `Lysdæmpning` channel is a per-depth
reading rather than a fitted profile coefficient, so it escapes *that* particular
artefact — but it inherits **4, schema conflation** instead: it is pooled across sonde
models and calibrations, and **5, unfilled field**, in that no cast carries the
instrument gain that would make two casts comparable.)

**And the target's own name is the last objection.** TEP is called *transparent* because
its refractive index is close to seawater's; it requires staining to be counted at all.
An optical proxy for it is therefore an attempt to detect, in a residual, the substance
chosen for having the weakest optical expression of anything in the water. Aggregated gel
— marine snow — does scatter, but turbidity already counts that, so the term is subtracted
out before R is formed.

## What building it anyway would and would not license

It **would** license a description: how much attenuation in inner Danish waters is
unexplained by the three sensors that exist, how that varies by season and station, and
whether the unexplained share has a shape. That is a worthwhile figure and nobody has it.

It **would not** license calling the residual gel, TEP, or fedtemøg. That is the shrinkage
move in `RESIDUAL.md` — naming the leftover after one item on its list — and here the list
demonstrably includes sonde calibration drift, residual CDOM beyond what FDOM tracks, and
spectral mismatch between a broadband attenuation channel and two narrow-band fluorometers.

## The smallest unblocking thing

**Thirty Alcian blue TEP samples, taken from the rosette on casts that already run the
four-sensor sonde suite, at any of the 211 stations that have carried FDOM since 2021.**
Not a survey — a calibration. Thirty paired points are enough to ask whether R carries any
TEP information at all, and the answer is informative in both directions: a correlation
turns 9,717 existing casts into a TEP series retrospectively, and no correlation retires
the optical route permanently and cheaply. The method is standard and costs a filter, a
dye and a spectrophotometer.

Second-smallest, and it does not substitute: fetch `ODA-TILFOERSEL`'s in-water nutrient
counterpart at station resolution, so that whatever gel proxy exists can be put against
N:P **skew** rather than nutrient load — the shape `J1` actually predicts, and the one no
error budget has a reason to imitate.
